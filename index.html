Voltage drop across cable sections

Use for a borehole pump or any circuit with different cable sizes. Lengths are one-way; the formula accounts for the return path in single-phase circuits.

1. Supply cable – DB / control box to borehole

2. Submersible extension cable – joint to motor lead

3. Factory-fitted motor lead

The short factory-fitted lead and extension cable are separate. If the cable is continuous all the way to the pump, enter it in section 2 and set section 3 to 0 m.

Calculation model: ΔV = factor × I × ρ(T) × Σ(length/area). Factor = 2 for single-phase and √3 for balanced three-phase; resistance component only. Copper ρ₂₀ = 0.0175 and aluminium ρ₂₀ = 0.0282 Ω·mm²/m; approximate temperature correction. This simplification is not a complete AC motor calculation.

Ohm's Law

V = voltage (volts) · I = current (amperes) · R = resistance (ohms)

How do I use the triangle? Cover the letter you want to calculate. Side-by-side letters are multiplied; stacked letters are divided.

Cover V: V = I × R
Cover I: I = V ÷ R
Cover R: R = V ÷ I

Where does it come from? Ohm's law describes the relationship between voltage, current and resistance in an ohmic conductor under suitable, constant conditions. The triangle is a memory aid for the same equation: V = I × R. Example: 12 V across a 6 Ω resistor gives I = 12 ÷ 6 = 2 A. Note: this simple model does not fully describe AC motor behaviour.

Power Law (Joule's Law)

The power triangle is a memory aid for P = V × I in a DC or purely resistive circuit. For AC loads with a power factor, use the separate AC calculator below.

P = power (watts) · V = voltage (volts) · I = current (amperes)

How do I use the triangle? Cover the letter you want to calculate. Multiply letters next to each other; divide letters above one another.

Cover P: P = V × I
Cover V: V = P ÷ I
Cover I: I = P ÷ V

Where does it come from? Electrical power is the rate at which electrical energy is transferred. For DC or purely resistive circuits, P = V × I. Example: At 230 V and 5 A, power is 1,150 W (1.15 kW).

Residential load-estimate method

Source and limitation: Based on the user-provided extract of SANS 10142-1 Edition 3.3, Annex C, page 298 (including Amendment 2 wording). Only the supplied extract has been reviewed. The diversity factors are examples, not mandatory defaults for every installation. This estimate does not establish a compliant cable or protective device.

Annex C: use actual luminaire rated input. For LED/electronic lamps without rated luminaire input, use rated lamp watts × 1.5. Socket options are 3 kW for the first 50 m², or 5 kW for the first 100 m² plus 1 kW for each further 100 m² or part. The supplied extract gives no rule for extending the 3 kW option beyond 50 m²; select the 100 m² method for larger areas. Example diversity: 50% lighting, heating, cooking, sockets; 100% water heaters and motors. Check actual nameplate input for motors and air conditioners.
